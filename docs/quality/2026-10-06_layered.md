# Layered renders (background first) — 2026-10-06

Idea: render the panel's place without people first, then paint the characters into
it. Expected: no split two-shots (one background), no costume bleed (one character
per pass), a better setting (nothing competes with it), and backgrounds that could be
reused across a scene. Expected cost: less interaction, seams, 2-3× the time.

Built as one ComfyUI graph that stays in latent space (`graphs.layered_per_character`):
a background sampler (`no humans, scenery`, the setting tags, people in the negative),
then one masked sampler per character over their area (the default bands or the
artist's placements), each with only that character's solo-panel prompt and their
reference (`IPAdapterAdvanced` with `attn_mask`).

## Measured: two-character panels, 8 seeds, against the current defaults

Sheet: `2026-10-06_layered.jpg` (wide two-shot; rows: current, layered per character,
layered together).

- **Per character** does what it was meant to: one shared background in every wide
  two-shot, real settings (the current renders sit on near-empty backgrounds), and
  solo poses like hands on hips follow better. But the character passes repaint whole
  boxes, so **band edges show as seams** in the sky in about half the seeds, **the
  second character (Akira) sometimes doesn't appear** ("saw solo"), and identity drops.
  The drag is gone: characters painted apart don't touch.
- **Together** (both characters in one pass over the union of their areas, with masked
  prompts and references) makes collages: fragments of panels and repeated figures.
  Dropped.
- **Denoise** of the character passes: 1.0 repainted each box from scratch (box-shaped
  seams), 0.7 left the plate dominant (the characters shrank or vanished). 0.85 is the
  least bad.

**Not adopted.** `defaults.layered.mode: per_character` stays as an opt-in prototype,
off by default.

## What would make it worth another try

- **Figure-shaped masks instead of boxes.** The seams come from repainting a box's
  background. A rough figure silhouette (from a pose, or from a first draft run
  through the person detector) would leave the plate alone around the character.
- **An eye-level plate.** "scenery" draws the place from a distance and often from
  above; the characters then don't stand in its perspective. The shot's framing and
  angle need to reach the plate prompt.
- **Scene continuity** (one plate for every panel of a scene) is the use this
  prototype is closest to, and it is untested: the rooftop suite has no
  same-background check.
